Corrections

If Wren shows something wrong about a product or misstates published guidance, tell us and it gets fixed.

Email engr.kamalnasir@gmail.com with the subject line Correction. A reply comes from the developer, usually within a couple of days.

For clinicians and public health bodies

If Wren misrepresents your guidance, or cites a document that has since been superseded, that is the most important correction we can receive and it will be prioritised. Please send the flag as it appears in the app and the current guidance you would expect it to reflect. Guidance corrections are applied ahead of the normal data build schedule.

For manufacturers and brand owners

If an ingredient list, a product name, or a flag shown in Wren is wrong or out of date for your product, we want to correct it rather than argue about it. Please send:

  • The product name and its barcode.
  • What Wren currently shows.
  • What the current label actually says, ideally with a photograph of the panel.

Corrections are applied in the next data build and shipped with the next app update. If a correction is urgent because the app is showing something materially misleading about your product, say so in the email and it will be prioritised.

How Wren assesses ingredients

Every flag in the app names the source it came from and links it, and the in-app Methodology screen explains what each category means. Wren reports what published guidance says; it does not rate brands, does not score products against each other on pregnancy grounds, and never states that an item is safe.

For shoppers

If a barcode resolves to the wrong product, or an ingredient list does not match the package in your hand, that report is just as useful. Include the barcode and what the package says.
