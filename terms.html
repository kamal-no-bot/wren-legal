Terms of Use

Please read this before relying on anything Wren tells you.

What Wren is

Wren reads product barcodes and printed ingredient lists and shows you what published guidance says about the ingredients it finds during pregnancy. It is an information tool built on public guidance from government and professional bodies, and every flag names the source it came from and links it on screen.

What Wren is not

  • It is not medical, obstetric, dietary, or nutritional advice.
  • It is not a verdict on whether any food, drink, or product is fit for you to consume or use.
  • It does not know your pregnancy, your history, or your risk factors.
  • It is not a substitute for reading the label or asking your midwife, obstetrician, or doctor.

Wren does not tell you a food is safe

This is the app's central rule rather than a disclaimer. Listeria risk depends on storage, handling, and preparation that no label records, mercury varies within a species, and recipes change without notice. Wren therefore reports what the published guidance says and never states that any item is safe to eat, drink, or apply. Where guidance is unclear or absent, it says that instead of inventing an answer.

In an emergency, or if something is wrong

If you feel unwell, believe you have eaten something that may harm your pregnancy, or have any symptom that worries you, contact your clinician or your local emergency service. Do not use this app to decide whether to seek care.

Accuracy of data

Pregnancy guidance is drawn from public sources including the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, the National Institutes of Health Office of Dietary Supplements, the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health, and the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations. Product and nutrition data comes from the United States Department of Agriculture FoodData Central, which is a voluntary industry submission database. All of it is a snapshot taken when the app version was built, and guidance may have been updated or a package reformulated since. If you find an error, please report it: see Corrections.

Where the guidance is written for a different country

The bundled product data is American and most of the cited guidance is issued by United States agencies. Advice on this subject genuinely differs between countries. If you are being cared for elsewhere, your own health service's advice is the one to follow.
